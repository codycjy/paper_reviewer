      "summary_comment": "This paper makes a solid theoretical and empirical contribution by extending the treatment of neural network symmetries from simple rescaling to full GL(h) symmetries present in transformers, using principled Riemannian geometry. The work correctly identifies that adaptive sharpness fails to capture transformer symmetries and proposes a mathematically rigorous solution. The recovery of significant correlations where adaptive sharpness found none is noteworthy. However, the work has practical limitations: the approach introduces computational and algorithmic complexity (full rank assumptions, Sylvester solvers, metric choices), and more critically, it reveals that the sign of correlation between sharpness and generalization varies unpredictably across tasks without explanation. While this is framed as an improvement (finding *some* correlation rather than none), it undermines the practical utility for regularization or theoretical understanding until the underlying phenomenon is explained. The evaluation, while competent, could be broader in scope (more architectures and datasets) and deeper in analysis (timing comparisons, sensitivity to hyperparameters, understanding of sign reversals). The paper makes sufficient advances to merit publication but leaves important questions unresolved."
