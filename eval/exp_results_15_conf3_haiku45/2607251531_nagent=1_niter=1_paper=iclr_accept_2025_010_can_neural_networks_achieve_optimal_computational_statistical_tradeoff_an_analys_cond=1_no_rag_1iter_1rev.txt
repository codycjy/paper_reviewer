The main result—showing that gradient-based training can achieve O_r(d^{s*/2}) sample complexity matching SQ lower bounds for any s* ≥ 1—is significant and resolves an open question. The weight perturbation technique is novel and technically sound. However, the paper has substantial limitations for practical deployment: (1) algorithms require knowledge of the generative exponent s*, construction of high-order Hermite polynomial activations, and potentially impractical support cover constructions; (2) experimental validation is limited to synthetic data at small scale; (3) polylogarithmic overheads are substantial but not well characterized; (4) several technical assumptions lack clear motivation or justification. The work is primarily valuable as a theoretical contribution to the computational learning theory community rather than a practical tool. The paper is well-written in its theoretical development, though some sections become dense and notation-heavy. For a top-tier venue, the combination of novel theoretical techniques, comprehensive characterization of the problem space, and rigorous proofs outweighs the practical limitations, warranting acceptance."
